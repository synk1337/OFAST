Sharp frames for velocity edits, depth of field you key over the round, a first-person gun that moves like CS2's, and one viewmodel for your whole movie.

**Download** `OFAST-Studio_1.0.6_x64-setup.exe` below and run it over your current version, or press **Update** in the Studio's bar (1.0.3 and later). The maps and rounds you already sent stay. **Send a round again** to get the new first-person gun and the Viewmodel panel in it.

## Sharp frames for velocity edits

**Render** has a **Motion blur** box. Untick it and every frame comes out sharp, a picture of its exact moment: ramp the speed in your editing software, then add the blur there (frame blending, RSMB, Twixtor). With 250 fps or more you get what HLAE gave you for this. A sharp render goes to its own folder (ending in `_sharp`).

## Depth of field over the round

The **Cameras** panel in Unreal has a **Depth of field** section. Put the playhead at a moment, pick what is sharp (**where the player looks**, a player, or a distance), set the **blur strength**, and press **Add focus key here**. Add more keys: the focus glides from one to the next (a focus pull from one player to another), on every camera, first person and cinematics alike. Two limits set how blurred things in front of the focus and behind it can get. Renders use them.

## The gun moves with the player

In first person the gun now bobs and sits lower while the player runs, sinks while they fall, and trails a little behind fast turns, as CS2's does (measured on CS2's own frames), on top of the recoil.

## One viewmodel for your movie

Every player's gun is now drawn with CS2's default viewmodel (field of view 68, offsets 2.5, 0, −1.5), so a movie made of several players' views looks like one game. The new **Viewmodel** panel in Unreal (opened by **Open in Unreal**, or **OFAST → Viewmodel**) changes it for every round: field of view, offsets, hand, how much the gun moves, or each player's own settings; **CS2's default** puts it back. The gun is drawn as CS2 draws it in a game; CS2's demo playback (and HLAE) shows guns a little smaller than that.
