Renders that fit your graphics card, four sizes from 720p to 4K, ProRes video beside the images, and a power setting that keeps your PC usable while Unreal renders.

**Download** `OFAST-Studio_1.0.11_x64-setup.exe` below and run it over your current version, or press **Update** in the Studio's bar (1.0.3 and later). The maps and rounds you already sent stay; nothing needs sending again.

## Renders fit your graphics card

- A 4K render used to need far more graphics memory than most cards have: it spilled into the PC's own memory, every frame took minutes and the whole PC crawled. Renders now take much less memory, and when a size still doesn't fit your card, Unreal shades the frames a little smaller and scales them up to the size you picked. The Studio's bar says when that happens.
- On a 12 GB card: 720p and 1080p are rendered whole, 2K at 85 % and 4K at 50 %. On 16 GB and more, every size should render whole (estimated from measurements on a 12 GB card).
- On a recent 12 GB card at full power, 1080p takes one to two seconds a frame, 2K and 4K about two and a half.
- On cards with less than 12 GB, renders still run short of memory, though far less than before; the bar warns about it.
- Unreal also needs 17 to 21 GB of the PC's own memory to render: close games and other big programs first.

## Sizes

The render sizes are now **720p**, **1080p**, **2K** (2560 × 1440) and **4K**, all rendered with the same quality. They replace Preview, Final and 4K; 720p is the quick one to check your cuts. The Studio remembers the size you picked.

## ProRes video

New **As** choice: **Images** (PNG and EXR frames, as before) or **ProRes video**: one Apple ProRes 4444 file (.mov) with every cut in it, visually lossless and a tenth of the images' size. It opens in Vegas Pro, Premiere, After Effects and DaVinci Resolve; checked in Vegas Pro 2026.

## Players layer

The players layer (the players alone and the world without them, for compositing) is now a switch, **Players layer**, off by default: a render without it is faster and takes much less memory. With it, a graphics card of 16 GB is needed to render 1080p whole. As ProRes video, the layer's files keep their transparency.

## Power

New **Power** setting: **Full** renders as fast as your PC goes; **Balanced** (the default) lets the graphics card rest a quarter of the time and keeps Unreal below your other programs, so the PC stays usable (about a third longer); **Low** rests the card half of the time: a third less power, cooler and quieter, almost twice as long. The frames are the same at any power.

## Disk space

The bar warns before a render that won't fit on your disk: a minute of 4K images is over 100 GB.

## Fixed

- A round that is no longer in Unreal (another round of the same map was sent after it) used to render all the same: a fixed view of the map with nobody in it. **Render** now stops at once and asks you to send the round again.
- In renders, the shade under ledges and lintels no longer looks darker and bluer than in Unreal's viewport (with the players layer switched on, it still does).

## Known issues

- Seen through the player's eyes, the gun kicks up more after each shot than in CS2.
- With the players layer, the players are lit without the light bounced from the walls around them: in a dark place they come out too dark.

Checked on CS2 1.41.9.6 (build 2000942).
