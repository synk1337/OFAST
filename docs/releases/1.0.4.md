Your own cinematic shots, renders at any frame rate for slow motion, a Look panel to change the lighting and the picture, and calmer charms.

**Download** `OFAST-Studio_1.0.4_x64-setup.exe` below and run it over your current version, or press **Update** in the Studio's bar (1.0.3 and later). The maps and rounds you already sent stay. Send a round again to get the changes in it.

## Your own cinematics

**Open in Unreal** now also opens the **Cameras** panel (also in Unreal's menu bar: **OFAST → Cameras**). It works like HLAE's campath, inside Unreal: fly the view to where the camera should be at a moment of the round (hold the right mouse button, W A S D, Q and E) and press **Add point here**, move on in time, fly, add the next point. OFAST makes a smooth camera move through your points, and the round cuts to it from the first point to the last.

- **Keep in frame** a player: the camera turns to keep them in the middle of the picture.
- **Follow** a player: the camera moves with them, smoothly, like a drone.
- **Focus on them**, with a **Depth of field** slider: the player sharp, the rest blurred.
- **Preview** plays the round through its cuts; **Earlier point** and **Later point** jump between your points.

Your cinematics are kept for each round: they come back when you send the round again or change its cameras, and **Render** includes them even if you closed Unreal without saving.

## Renders at any frame rate

**Render** has a new **fps** box. 60 plays at normal speed. For slow motion in your editing software (Vegas, Premiere, After Effects), render more, 250 for example: slowed to a quarter, the clip still has over 60 frames a second, each a real moment of the round, none blended from its neighbours. Any number from 10 to 1000; the render takes longer in proportion. Import the images at the frame rate you rendered, then slow the clip down there.

## The Look panel

**Open in Unreal** also opens the **Look** panel (**OFAST → Look**): sliders for the map's look, seen live in the viewport.

- **Sun**: height (low for a golden hour), direction, strength, colour, how bright the shade is.
- **Atmosphere** (realistic look): haze, light beams.
- **Camera**: brightness, depth of field, motion blur.
- **Colour and film**: warm or cool, saturation, contrast, vignette, film grain; bloom and lens flare in the realistic look.

Each map keeps its own settings, for every round you send there, and renders use them.

## Fixed
